7 Best App Blockers for Android (2026)

Published July 16, 2026 · NexusDrift Studio

We compared the most-used Android focus and app-blocking tools on three things that actually matter: privacy, price, and whether they survive real willpower. Here's the ranking.

1. Digital Wellbeing (built-in)

Google's native tool. Free, on-device, no install. You can set app timers and "Bedtime" mode. Catch: it's easy to bypass with one tap ("ignore 24h"), and there's no way to make limits stick without willpower.

Verdict: Great baseline, weak enforcement.

2. StayFree

Strong usage analytics and over-limit alerts. Free tier covers most needs; Pro adds more. Tracking is relatively light. Like Digital Wellbeing, the override is one tap away.

Verdict: Best stats dashboard, same override problem.

3. StepShield Pro

The only blocker on this list that makes screen time something you earn. You block distracting apps, set a daily step goal; apps unlock only after you walk. Contains ads, no paid tier, no analytics, no cloud. Because unlock is tied to movement — not a button you can tap — it defeats the "just disable it" workaround that kills every other blocker.

Verdict: Best for people who've failed at "just delete the app." Truly private.

4. Forest

Gamified: plant a virtual tree that grows while you don't use your phone, dies if you leave. Motivating, but requires account and syncs to cloud. Paid model also gates some features.

Verdict: Fun, but cloud-dependent.

5. BlockSite

Quick to set up, blocks both sites and apps, has a focus mode. Collects data under a privacy-focused policy.

Verdict: Convenient, but telemetry-heavy.

6. AppBlock

Excellent scheduled, location-based, recurring block profiles. Light tracking. Interface is denser than casual users want.

Verdict: Best rule-based scheduling for power users.

7. Cold Turkey

The gold standard for hardcore locking on desktop, but its Android support is limited compared to its Windows/macOS depth. Minimal tracking.

Verdict: Use it on desktop; don't rely on it on Android.

How to choose: three questions

Ask three questions of any blocker: Does it need an account? Does it send your usage to a server? Is the "free" version really free, or does it nag you into a paid tier that quietly disables your block list?

StepShield Pro is the only entry here that answers no / no / yes-free-forever across the board.
